Dropdown Time Tax

Alaska is second in every alphabetical list of US state names, so Alaskans barely scroll. Wyoming is last. Pick where you live below, in a real dropdown, and see what your place in the alphabet costs you over a lifetime. Then see what your place on the map costs you in commuting, which turns out to be a much bigger bill.

2 · The waiting tax

Crowded places make you wait. The waiting time we can actually source for every state is the commute: the Census Bureau's American Community Survey asks workers how long their trip to work takes. Some of that is moving, a lot of it in dense places is queues, lights and traffic. It is a proxy for waiting, not a stopwatch on queues.

How the numbers are made

Scroll tax per pick MODEL

tax(i) = Σ_mode share_mode · [ T_mode(i) − T_mode(1) ] mouse: T(i) = max(0, i − V) / R + b · log2(min(i, V) + 1) (scroll the rows hidden below the popup, then a Fitts'-law point at a row) keyboard: T(i) = k(i) · K k(i) = fewest letters typed before the browser's type-ahead lands on entry i touch: T(i) = (i − 1) · c (scroll-wheel picker on phones; distance-proportional) autofill: T(i) = 0 lifetime = tax(i) · picks per year · form-filling years everyone = tax(i) · picks per year · population

Only the part of the time that depends on position counts as the tax. Opening the menu, reading the label and clicking cost the same for Alabama and Wyoming, so they cancel. The range shown under the lifetime number moves every estimate to its low and high end at once.

Waiting tax MODEL

extra commute per lifetime = (m_state − m_min) · 2 trips · commuting days per year · working years fit line: m = a + b · log10(density) (ordinary least squares over 50 states + DC + Puerto Rico)

What this leaves out

  • Many sites pin the United States, Canada or the UK to the top of country lists, or use a search box. Tick "site pins my choice" to see that case.
  • Type-ahead is modelled as typing a prefix. Pressing the same first letter repeatedly to cycle through entries is another real strategy and is not modelled.
  • Visible rows default to 20, the popup limit in Chromium's source. Safari and Chrome on macOS show a native menu that fits as many rows as the screen allows, so try 40 for a Mac.
  • World Bank population has no Taiwan figure and nothing for most small territories, so "everyone there" is blank for those.
  • Commute times are state averages for workers who did not work from home. Within a state the spread between a rural and a city commute is wider than the spread between states.
